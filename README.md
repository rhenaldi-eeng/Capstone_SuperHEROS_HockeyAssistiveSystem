# Capstone_SuperHEROS_HockeyAssistiveSystem

Control Systems Code for Capstone Project.

In Layman's terms:
Medical Need: Participants of the SuperHEROS hockey program with cognitive and physical challenges who use wheelchairs who are unable to use standard hockey hockey equipment 

Current Solutions: Currently, parents and the volunteers of the organization rigidly attach hockey sticks to any of the mobility aids available by the provider (either their own wheelchairs or front mounted or kaye trainers provided by the organization). 

Technical Barriers: These rigidly attached hockey sticks do not provide the independence for the participant, which leaves them to rely on the volunteer aid to shoot and pass.

Goal: Our goal is to do develop a button controlled assistive hockey device, giving the SuperHEROS participants more interactions and independence in the game 

SuperHEROS offers programs for young people with cognitive and physical challenges to play hockey. Participants who use wheelchairs have various motor impairments and are unable to use a standard hockey stick due to their reduced physical ability and the obstruction from their mobility aid, which includes personal wheelchairs, front-mounted walkers and Kaye trainers. Currently, participants of the program are unable to shoot the puck on their own, making it difficult for  them to be involved in the game as users must rely on volunteers to pass and shoot the puck by pushing or spinning their manual mobility aid .To enable a sense of participation, parents and volunteers have rigidly attached hockey sticks to the front of  participants’ mobility aids. However, this still does not provide the participants with true shooting independence. This issue remains unaddressed, as many may not see an economic reason to develop a solution for such a niche group. Our project aims to develop a button-controlled assistive hockey device that allows players to actively engage in the game through independent shooting control. This will increase their independence and interaction during play.
